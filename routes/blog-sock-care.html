Caring for Your Cotton Socks

Good cotton socks earn their keep over dozens of wears, not just one. A few small habits are the difference between a pair that looks new after a year and one that's gone thin and grey after a month.

Wash inside out, in cold or lukewarm water

Turning socks inside out before washing protects the outer surface — where the colour and any ribbing sit — from the friction of the wash cycle. Cold or lukewarm water (avoid hot) keeps cotton fibres from shrinking and helps colours hold longer.

Skip the fabric softener

It feels like it should help, but fabric softener coats cotton fibres in a way that reduces breathability and can quietly wear down elastic over time — exactly the two things you don't want to lose in a good pair of socks.

Air dry when you can

Tumble dryers are the single biggest reason elastic cuffs lose their grip early. High heat breaks down the elastane fibres woven into the cuff and heel. If you must use a dryer, go for the lowest heat setting and pull socks out while still slightly damp.

Wash similar colours together

Simple, but it's the most common reason white or light socks pick up a dull grey tint within a few washes. Keep your lighter pairs separate from darker items.

Pair and store them properly

Folding socks into each other stretches the cuff over time. A better habit: fold the top of one sock over the pair, or simply store them side by side in a drawer divider — either keeps the elastic in its original shape for longer.

None of this is complicated — it's mostly about avoiding heat and friction, the two things that age cotton fastest. Get those right, and a well-made pair of socks will outlast the ones that weren't.
